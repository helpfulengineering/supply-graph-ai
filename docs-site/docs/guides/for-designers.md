# For designers

You have designed some open hardware and want people who aren't you to find out
they could build it. This page is the short path.

## You don't install anything

OHM runs as **nodes**: copies of the software that each hold their own records
and share them with each other. You don't need to run one. You become a
**member** of a node that already exists — the one at openhardwaremanager.org,
or one your community runs — and add your design there. A browser is all it
takes.

Installing OHM is for organisations that want to hold their own records on
infrastructure they control. If that's not you, skip
[Run your own node](run-your-own-node.md) entirely.

## Become a member

Reading OHM needs no account. Adding a design does. Whether you can register
yourself is the node's choice — `open`, `invite`, or `closed` — and that
choice overrides the security mode. See [Join a node](join-a-node.md) for what
you get and what to keep safe.

## Add your design

If your design lives in a public GitHub or GitLab repository, choose **Add your
design** on the home page and give it the repository URL. OHM reads the
repository into a draft record — title, licence, manufacturing processes, bill
of materials — and you review and correct it before anything is kept.

Reading the repository with a language model gives a fuller draft, and needs an
account on that node. Without one, OHM extracts what it can by rule and leaves
the rest for you to fill in.

- [Import a design from a repository URL](import-from-a-url.md) — the review
  step in detail, and what to do when it doesn't work.
- [Add a design](add-a-design.md) — the other ways in, if you already have an
  OKH file or would rather fill in a form.

## Who sees it

A new design starts private: only you can see it until you change that. When
you're ready, widen its visibility and it travels to the nodes that follow
yours. See [Who can see your data](who-can-see-your-data.md).
